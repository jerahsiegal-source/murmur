Yappr: five directions for the new look

The job: someone who just installed Yappr can dictate in their first minute, and it looks good enough that they keep it open.

Fixed in every direction: the Yappr name, Skip the dog, and the brand colours (butter, cream, bark brown). All of today's features stay: Ctrl+Win dictation, hands-free, Alt+Shift+Z re-paste, meeting notes, read-aloud, dictionary and history. Nothing loads from the internet. What changes is layout, density, type, how colour is used, and how you get around.

A · Expected

Windows Native

Use the patterns Windows 11 users already know: a left sidebar, setting rows with a plain one-line explanation, and controls on the right. Yappr's brown and butter replace Windows blue. Nothing to learn.

BEST AT

Trust and clarity. Every setting has a plain "what this does" line, and there's no learning curve.

RISK

Safe, but forgettable. It looks like any other Windows utility, and Skip only shows up as an icon.

B · Bold

Skip Leads

Skip is the interface. The bar is Skip: he perks up while listening and wags when the text lands. Big butter surfaces, chunky type and one setting per row with plenty of space. First run is a short chat with Skip.

BEST AT

Being memorable and friendly. New users learn by doing, and the brand finally does real work.

RISK

Can feel too cute in a work meeting or on a shared screen. Needs a "calm mode" and real illustration work.

C · Borrowed

Palette

Borrowed from Raycast and VS Code's command palette. There are no tabs: Yappr opens as one search box that finds any setting, word, note or past dictation. The bar is tiny until you talk, then grows into a heads-up strip that shows your words as you say them.

BEST AT

Speed, and staying out of the way. Live text while you talk builds trust that it heard you.

RISK

New users don't know what to search for. Needs good suggestions when the box is empty. Dark-first may feel heavy to some.

D

Today

Yappr gets a home screen. It opens to your next meeting, a timeline of today's dictations and notes, and a setup checklist until you're done. Settings move behind a gear. The bar shows a countdown to your next meeting.

BEST AT

Showing what Yappr did for you, so it's easy to come back to. Setup is guided by the checklist, not a wizard you can't return to.

RISK

The most to build. Stats like "time saved" need an honest way to count them, or they should go.

E

Notebook

Your words come first. The window is a calm two-pane reader for meeting notes and past dictations, set in Atkinson Hyperlegible at reading size. Settings are one scrolling page you rarely visit. The bar is soft cream with the shortcut written out.

BEST AT

Reading and using meeting notes. Very readable, low effort to look at all day.

RISK

Quiet. Settings and setup feel secondary, and someone who only dictates rarely opens the window at all.
